The floor: maximise government help

Claim in full

Free, matched money — in rough order of size. Autism is an explicitly accepted disability under GOAL+; all of these need the Disability Verification Form (DVF) from a registered healthcare professional.

Up to $10,000 matched

GOAL+ dollar-for-dollar top-up

The Government matches your contributions into his Special Needs Trust account dollar-for-dollar, up to $10,000, across a 5-year window. Runs 1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2031. SNTC setup fees are separately 90–100% subsidised by MSF.

Who: SC/PR with permanent disability (autism qualifies); per capita household income ≤ $3,600 (or annual value of property ≤ $21,000 if PCHI is $0).

sgenable.sg → GOAL+ (apply)
$5,000 setup covered

Community Chest seed

If he has no trust account yet, Community Chest sponsors the full $5,000 initial deposit needed to open the Special Needs Trust account, on top of the GOAL+ match.

Who: families joining GOAL+ without an existing SNT account.

sgenable.sg → Special Needs Trust
$2,000/yr matched · $20,000 life

MRSS — CPF top-up match

The Government matches cash top-ups to his CPF Special Account dollar-for-dollar, up to $2,000 a year, lifetime cap $20,000. From 1 Jan 2026 this is open to persons with disabilities of all ages — no need to wait for 55.

Who: SC/PR with verified disability, below 55 → top up SA; 55+ → top up RA. Anyone (family, employer) can top up.

cpf.gov.sg → MRSS
Subsidised court filing

ADAP — assisted deputyship

If he lacks mental capacity, someone must be his legal deputy to hold accounts and investments for him. ADAP makes the Family Court application simpler and cheaper, with a guided filing through MINDS and a Mental Capacity Assessment (valid 3 years) at no cost.

Who: child aged 18–21 graduating from a SPED school, or enrolled in an MSF-funded Day Activity Centre / Sheltered Workshop; uncontested cases.

enablingguide.sg → ADAP
CPF → monthly payouts

SNSS — Special Needs Savings Scheme

His parents nominate their CPF monies to him; after they pass, SNTC disburses it to him as monthly payouts at an amount the parents pre-set, until the savings run out. This is the parents' side of the same trust — set it up alongside the SNT.

Who: parents of a child with special needs making a CPF nomination.

sgenable.sg → SNSS
Monthly income support

ComCare

If household income is low, MSF's ComCare provides ongoing help: Short-to-Medium Term Assistance (income ≤ $1,900/mo or ≤ $650 per capita) and Long Term Assistance for those permanently unable to work. Applied for at the nearest Social Service Office.

Who: low-income households with a member who cannot work or needs care.

msf.gov.sg → ComCare

Good to have: safety leg + growth leg

Build after the floor

Once every matched dollar is claimed, these two layers do the compounding.

Safety leg

Top up the SA account beyond the match

CPF Special Account earns a government-guaranteed 4% p.a. — the risk-free floor of the whole plan. Top up above the MRSS-matched $2,000/year (extra top-ups just earn the interest, un-matched), and the money stays withdrawable for retirement payouts via his own CPF. Cash here is never at market risk.

Rule of thumb: floor first (GOAL+ + MRSS matched amounts), then extra SA, then markets.

cpf.gov.sg → MRSS details
Growth leg

S&P 500 under will → trustee

Build the equity sleeve while the parents are living — an S&P 500 index ETF (Ireland-domiciled, e.g. CSPX/VUAA class, via Endowus or IBKR to avoid US estate-tax exposure) DCA'd monthly in his own account, or held by his deputy if he lacks capacity. On the parents' passing, the will routes cash and accounts into the trustee, and the Letter of Intent controls how it is disbursed to him — monthly, for life.

Structure: the will feeds the trust; the trust (SNTC) holds the safe money with government-guaranteed principal; the equity sleeve stays in the investment account the trustee or deputy controls.

supportgowhere.life.gov.sg → Special Needs Trust
Honest caveat on the S&P inside the trust: funds held by the Special Needs Trust are invested by the Public Trustee with principal guaranteed by the Government — safe, but not equities. To keep S&P 500 exposure for him, the index sleeve must live in the brokerage account (his own, or his deputy's), with the will simply bequeathing it to the trust or naming the trust as beneficiary.

Order of action

Do it in this sequence
  1. Verify the disability (DVF). A registered healthcare professional completes the Disability Verification Form through SG Enable — this unlocks GOAL+, MRSS and every scheme below. Check existing status on SupportGoWhere with Singpass.
  2. Settle mental capacity. Doctor or psychologist assesses: has capacity → LPA form at 21. Lacks capacity → deputyship, via ADAP if eligible (18–21 SPED / DAC / SW) or the standard Family Court process with Legal Aid Bureau / Community Justice Centre.
  3. Open the Special Needs Trust, collect GOAL+. $5,000 initial deposit (Community Chest sponsors it), then top up within the 5-year window for the dollar-for-dollar match up to $10,000.
  4. Top up CPF SA every year, collect MRSS. $2,000/year in, $2,000/year matched; extra top-ups above that still earn 4%.
  5. Start the S&P 500 sleeve and write the will. Monthly DCA into an Ireland-domiciled index ETF; the will names the trust so everything passes to the trustee on the parents' passing. Add SNSS (CPF nomination) the same day.
